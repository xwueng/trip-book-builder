This is the facilitator copy — setup, step scripts with notes, and checklists. Students get a separate Handbook: same steps, plain language, no notes.

The objective of this lab is to show where AI stands today. The trip book is the project, but the real point is watching Claude work: what it gets right on its own, what it has to infer. Encourage students to notice this as they build — the friction is the lesson, not something to apologize for.

Tap the small + square next to any heading below to open that section — tap it again to close it.

1. Class size and student devices
  • Class size: 6–10 students, working independently at their own pace.
  • Class length: 90 minutes.
  • Student devices: a laptop (recommended), tablet, or phone per student.
The setup steps: students create their own Claude account and Project — a real, if short, task. Budget 10–15 minutes each. Phone verification (below) is the most common snag, so build in some slack.

2. Before the first class — note to students
Bring your photos on your phone (or wherever they are). Pick one trip, and think of a title and the dates. No need to move your photos anywhere first — we'll upload them straight from your device in class. If you don't have a Gmail address to sign up with, bring a mobile phone that can receive text messages, even if you're working on a laptop — it's used once, to verify your new account.

3. Before the first class — facilitator prep list
  • (Before Step 1) Students without a Gmail address reminded to bring a mobile phone that can receive text messages, even if working on a laptop
  • The example book ready on the projector
  • Handouts printed in large type

4. Setting up each student's project
Students do this themselves, following the steps below — either as a pre-step slot or as the start of Step 1.

Part 1 — Create a Claude account
  1. Open a browser and go to claude.ai.
  2. Click Sign up. Choose "Continue with Google" (fastest, if they have a Google/Gmail account) or "Continue with email".
  3. If using email: enter the email address, then check that inbox for a message from Claude and click the verification link (or enter the code shown). Claude will then ask for a phone number and text a code to enter.
    This step trips people up. Phone verification only applies if a student signs up with email, not Google — it's how Claude confirms they're a real person. It needs a real mobile number; landlines, Google Voice, and other app-generated numbers are rejected. Students who sign up with Google skip this step entirely.
  4. Once the Claude chat screen appears, the account is ready.

Part 2 — Create a Project
  1. Click Projects in the left-hand menu (or go to claude.ai/projects).
  2. Click + New Project.
  3. Name the Project (e.g. "My Trip Books") and confirm.

Part 3 — Add the rules
  1. Copy this line exactly as written — click Copy, or select it yourself:
    Build trip book by following the spec in https://xwueng.github.io/trip-book-builder/trip_book_spec.html
  2. Inside the project, find Project instructions (same name on desktop and the mobile app), paste the line into that box, then click Save.
    No download or attachment needed — students don't have to save anything to their device for this step.

Once setup is done, students just upload photos with the + button — the project's instructions handle the rest: starting the book, asking for a title and dates, adding pages, asking about each photo, skip logic, facts, and sizing.

5. Building the trip book — the three steps
Each numbered step is a plain instruction to say to the student. Say one, wait until they've done it, then say the next. The shaded notes below steps are for you — don't read them aloud.

Step 1 — Start your book and add all your photos
  1. Open Claude in the browser — the student sets up their own account and Project; see "Setting up each student's project" above. (Budget 10–15 minutes.)
  2. Click the + at the bottom of the chat.
  3. Choose 4–6 photos to start with. (Ctrl+click for 4–6, or tap each one on a phone — steer them away from "Select All," which can grab the whole camera roll. A handful is enough to see the book take shape; more can go in later.)
  4. Click the icon to send them.
  5. Claude will ask for a title and the trip dates. Students can answer, or choose to skip — Claude will work from the photo dates instead. (Worth letting at least one student try "skip," so the class sees Claude infer the dates on its own.)
  6. Watch each photo become a page.
  7. Flip through your book on the screen.
If a device won't upload, open Claude in that device's own browser and use + there. The account, project, and spec file are only set up once — later steps just reopen the same project.

Step 2 — Put it in order and polish

Ask Claude to make any of these changes — students just type or say it in plain language.

What you want to doWhat to type/say
Change the order"Move the bridge photo to the front."
Remove a photo"Remove the blurry photo on page 4."
Add a fact"Add a fact about the Cliffs of Moher."
Confirm a guessed locationSee a "confirm venue" tag on a photo? Claude inferred the spot — tell it the real place: "That's actually Fisherman's Wharf."
Fix the wording"Change the line on the castle photo to…"

Flip through and check it reads the way you want.

Words to help students remember

Students don't need fancy words. One true sentence is plenty. Finish one of these for a photo:

  • "This was the moment we…"
  • "I'll always remember the…"
  • "We didn't expect…"
  • "The best part of this day was…"
  • "We laughed because…"
  • "This is where we…"

Or students can just ask Claude: "Give me a question about this photo."

Step 3 — Download and share it

The trip book downloads as an HTML file by default. Check the table below for the file your recipient needs — if you need a PDF, just ask Claude to make one.

Viewing deviceWhat file to downloadWhat to do
Android phoneHTML (default)Click Download to get the HTML file.
Windows or Mac computerHTML (default)Click Download to get the HTML file.
iPhone or iPadPDFType or say "Please generate a PDF file," then download the file Claude gives you.
Saving the HTML page as a PDF yourself (through the browser's print menu) does not produce a correct PDF. Students should always ask Claude to generate the PDF for them.
Downloading is optional — only for students who want a copy or want to send it to family.
  1. To send it to family: attach the right file to an email, a text message, or a messaging app.
    If email won't let the file through (photo-heavy books can get large), a USB drive or a cloud drive link (Google Drive, Dropbox) works too.
  2. Show the class your cover and one favourite page.

6. Watch for
  • Phone verification stalls — wrong number type (landline, Google Voice, VoIP), or the code takes a few minutes to arrive
  • Students mistyping or only partly pasting the Project-instructions line — check it matches exactly before moving on
  • Students who can't find Project instructions in their project — point them to it directly
  • Trouble uploading photos from a student device — open Claude in that device's own browser and use + there
  • A "confirm venue" tag left on a page — remind the student to just tell Claude the real place
  • Quiet students who are stuck — circulate and check screens
  • Remind students they can add photos from home anytime, by reopening their project

7. What Claude AI does and doesn't

Worth sharing with students as a plain-language summary of what's happening behind the scenes:

#What Claude does for youHow
1Builds your trip bookFollows your instructions to turn photos into a flip-through book
2Reads your photosDescribes what's in each photo and infers the place; flags any it's unsure about so you can confirm
3Edits on requestMakes the change you ask for in chat — moves a photo, changes the cover color, makes the fonts bigger, and more
4Lets you download and share itGenerates a downloadable file whenever you're ready — HTML by default, or a PDF on request

Limited by: platform support — iPhone and iPad don't open local HTML files as live pages; direct students to the PDF instead (see the table in Step 3).

Doesn't: generate a perfect trip book. Expect occasional misalignment, truncated text, or rough spots — worth having students check it over before sharing, and a good discussion point on where AI still needs a human check.
